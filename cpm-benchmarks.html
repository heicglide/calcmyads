CPM benchmarks 2026: platforms, verticals and seasonality

Reported CPM and CTR ranges for the major advertising platforms, with the sources listed, plus a method for building your own benchmark from your account data.

The short version: in 2026 US inventory, most social and display campaigns land between $4 and $15 CPM. LinkedIn runs $30–$100. Q4 costs 20–30% more than the annual average. Your own account history beats every number on this page.

How to read these numbers. They are aggregated ranges reported across 2026 industry benchmark studies, not measurements from our own ad accounts. Platform averages move with the auction, so treat each range as a starting hypothesis and replace it with your account's actual CPM within two weeks.

Vertical cost tiers

Within one platform, the vertical moves CPM more than any other single factor. Directional tiers, derived from the platform ranges above and the competitive intensity of each vertical:

TierTypical verticalsWhy
Low ($4–$9)Broad retail, entertainment, apps, local servicesLarge audiences, many placements, moderate competition
Mid ($9–$20)Ecommerce with defined audiences, health and wellness, education, travelTargeting narrows the audience; seasonal peaks
High ($20–$60)Finance, insurance, B2B software, professional servicesHigh customer value attracts heavy competition
Very high ($60+)Legal services in competitive US metros, some financial productsCase or contract value justifies aggressive bidding

Two advertisers can report the same $12 CPM and be in completely different businesses. What matters is CPM relative to the value of a customer, not the absolute number.

Seasonality: the US advertising year

PeriodCost pressureWhat usually happens
January – FebruaryLowestPost-holiday demand resets; cheap inventory, lower conversion rates
March – JuneModerateStable CPMs; good period for testing creative
July – AugustModerateSummer softness in some verticals, back-to-school spikes in others
September – OctoberRisingAdvertisers load Q4 budgets; CPM climbs
November – DecemberHighestHoliday and BFCM competition; CPMs commonly 20–30% above the annual average

Planning consequences: lock in creative and landing pages before October, expect your cost per acquisition to rise in November even if nothing in the account changed, and compare performance year over year rather than month over month.

Build your own benchmark in four steps

  1. Pull 90 days of platform data by campaign: impressions, clicks, spend, conversions, revenue.
  2. Segment by objective and placement. Blending awareness and conversion campaigns produces a number that describes nothing.
  3. Compute the four core rates: CPM, CTR, conversion rate and cost per acquisition. Add ROAS if you have revenue data.
  4. Compare like with like. Same platform, same objective, same quarter. Then set your target as the median of your best-performing month, not the average of everything.

Use the CPM calculator, CPC calculator and ROAS calculator for the arithmetic at each step.

Methodology and sources

Ranges on this page aggregate publicly reported 2026 benchmark studies and platform cost guides. Where sources disagreed, the wider range is shown. No figure here is derived from our own ad accounts, and none is a guarantee of future cost.

  • Google Ads and paid search benchmarks: WordStream industry benchmark reporting.
  • Social platform costs: Hootsuite's social media advertising cost reporting.
  • Meta CPM and ROAS benchmarks: published Meta ads benchmark studies (2026).
  • Platform CPM comparison ranges: independent CPM benchmark trackers.
  • TikTok and LinkedIn cost ranges: platform-specific 2026 cost guides.
  • CTR by channel: cross-channel CTR reporting.
  • Ecommerce ROAS benchmarks: published ecommerce ROAS and CVR studies.
