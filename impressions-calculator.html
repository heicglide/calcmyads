How to calculate impressions

Impressions = (budget ÷ CPM) × 1,000

Impressions are counted every time an ad is served, so the same person can generate several. The formula simply converts money into the volume of ad servings that money buys at the current price of 1,000 impressions.

Impressions are not reach

This is the most common planning error. Impressions count ad servings; reach counts unique people; frequency is how many times each person saw the ad on average:

Frequency 1 is only realistic for very broad, short campaigns. In practice, US social campaigns aimed at a defined audience usually land between 1.5 and 3, so treat your impression estimate as an upper bound on reach and expect it to shrink by a third or more.

How many impressions do you actually need?

  • Brand awareness: plan for at least 3 impressions per person in the target audience. Below that, recall effects are weak.
  • Direct response: impressions matter less than conversion volume. Aim for enough clicks to reach 50 conversions per week per campaign, then work back: impressions needed = conversions ÷ conversion rate ÷ CTR.
  • Retargeting: the audience size caps reach, so frequency rises quickly. Cap frequency and refresh creative instead of buying more impressions.

For the click side of that calculation, use the CTR calculator; for the cost side, use the ad cost calculator.

Why delivered impressions differ from the estimate

  • Auction prices move. CPM is not fixed. A higher price in the days you are live means fewer impressions for the same budget.
  • Pacing. Platforms spread delivery across the flight, so a campaign may under-deliver early and catch up later.
  • Audience size. If your audience is small, the platform cannot serve more impressions without pushing frequency up.
  • Ad review and learning. New creatives spend a day or two in review and learning, which reduces delivered volume.
  • Invalid traffic filtering. Platforms remove some impressions before reporting, so billed volume and reported volume can differ slightly.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate impressions from a budget?

Divide the budget by the CPM, then multiply by 1,000. A $3,000 budget at a $12 CPM is ($3,000 ÷ $12) × 1,000 = 250,000 impressions.

How many impressions can I get for $1,000?

It depends on your CPM: 250,000 at $4, 125,000 at $8 and 50,000 at $20. Most US social campaigns sit between $5 and $15 CPM, so 65,000–200,000 impressions is the typical range.

Are impressions the same as people reached?

No. Impressions count every time the ad is served, including repeat views by the same person. Reach counts unique people. Reach equals impressions divided by average frequency.

What is a good frequency?

Two to three impressions per person is a common working range for awareness in the US. Higher frequency with flat or falling CTR means the audience is tired of the ad and you should refresh creative rather than buy more impressions.

Why are impressions higher than views?

A view is a qualified impression — usually an impression where the ad was actually visible, or a video watched past a threshold. Impressions count every serving, so view counts are always lower.

Do impressions include my own visits?

Ad platform impression counts exclude your own activity in most cases, but website impression data in analytics tools can include internal traffic unless you filter it.

Should I buy more impressions or better targeting?

Better targeting, almost always. Doubling impressions at the same conversion rate doubles cost, while a better audience or stronger creative improves both CTR and conversion rate at the same spend.
