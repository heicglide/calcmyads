What matters specifically on WordPress

  • Page speed is usually the biggest handicap. Plugin-heavy WordPress sites often load slowly on mobile, which raises CPC on search (through landing page experience) and lowers conversion rate on social. Test the speed of the exact landing page you send paid traffic to, not the homepage.
  • Do not install a tracking plugin stack blindly. GA4 through Site Kit or a single analytics plugin, plus the Meta pixel if you run Meta ads, is enough. Conflicting plugins double-count conversions and make ROAS unreliable.
  • Check WooCommerce checkout for friction. Guest checkout, a single page instead of a multi-step form, saved payment methods and visible shipping costs all raise conversion rate more than any bid change.
  • Watch for plugin conflicts on landing pages. Pop-up, chat and cookie plugins are the most common cause of layout shifts that push the buy button off-screen on mobile.
  • Keep a staging copy of the landing page. Test headline, offer and form length there, then push the winner live. Small WordPress sites rarely get enough traffic for frequent split tests.
  • Cache, but exclude the cart and checkout. Caching those pages breaks sessions and can cost you orders.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I spend on ads for a WordPress site?

Enough to gather data: $500–$1,500 per month for a first test, then scale from a revenue or lead goal. Keep each campaign funded to about 50 conversions per week, otherwise the platform cannot optimise reliably.

How do I track ad conversions on WordPress?

Install GA4 (Site Kit or a single analytics plugin) and your ad platform's pixel, mark the thank-you page or order confirmation as a conversion, and use UTM tags on ad URLs. Reconcile platform-reported conversions against WooCommerce orders weekly.

Does WordPress page speed affect my ad costs?

Yes. Slow landing pages lower conversion rate, which raises your effective cost per acquisition, and on Google Search they can raise CPC through landing page experience. Compress images, limit plugins on the landing page and test the exact URL you advertise.

What ROAS should a WooCommerce store target?

Work from break-even ROAS: 1 divided by gross margin. A 40% margin store breaks even at 2.50x and should target above that plus a buffer for returns and fees. Reported ecommerce medians sit around 2x–3x.

Should I use Google Ads or Meta for a WordPress site?

Google captures existing demand and usually converts at a higher rate; Meta creates demand with cheaper impressions. Start with the channel that matches how your customers already find you, then add the other once tracking is reliable.

Is this calculator the same as a general ad spend calculator?

The arithmetic is the same. This page adds the WordPress-specific checks that most often change the outcome: landing page speed, plugin conflicts on the checkout, and conversion tracking reliability.
